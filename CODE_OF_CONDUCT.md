# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are acting in good faith. Criticise code, not the person who wrote it.

Most of what happens here will be someone saying "this URL form didn't work" or "step 4 doesn't match what I see on screen". Those people are doing the project a favour, often while frustrated, and they deserve a helpful answer regardless of how the report is phrased.

## What is expected

- **Welcome people who are not experts.** Later is meant to be deployable by someone who doesn't write code. "Read the docs" is not an answer if the docs were unclear — that's a bug, and it's the maintainer's to fix.
- **Be specific and be kind.** "This will break on Workers because `node:crypto` isn't available there" is useful. "This is obviously wrong" is not.
- **Accept a no.** Some things are deliberately not built, and the reasoning is in [PLAN.md's anti-goals](PLAN.md#anti-goals--deliberately-not-built) and the ADRs. Arguing the reasoning is welcome. Repeatedly reopening a settled decision is not.
- **Respect people's privacy.** Never ask anyone to share a token, a client secret, or a refresh token in a public thread. Nobody needs them to help, and asking normalises a dangerous habit.

## What is not acceptable

Harassment, personal attacks, discriminatory or sexualised language, deliberate misgendering, publishing anyone's private information, or sustained disruption. Also: pressuring the maintainer to add scraping, cookie replay, or anything else that would violate a platform's terms of service and expose everyone who deploys this.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Email **alepotger@gmail.com** with what happened and links if you have them. Reports are handled privately, and you will not be asked to confront the other person.

Responses run from a private word, through editing or removing comments, to a permanent ban for anything serious or repeated. This is a small project with one maintainer, so expect a best-effort response within a week — not a moderation team.

If the problem is with the maintainer's own conduct, GitHub's [Community Guidelines](https://docs.github.com/site-policy/github-terms/github-community-guidelines) and abuse reporting apply, and using them is entirely legitimate.
